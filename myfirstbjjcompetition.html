A Guide for Restigouche Dojo Athletes

Congratulations on taking the step to compete in your first Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu tournament. Competition is a great way to test your skills, gain experience, and grow as a martial artist. This guide will help you prepare physically and mentally so you can compete with confidence.

Key Areas to Focus On

Clear Goals

Know why you are competing and what success looks like for you.

Game Plan

Build a simple strategy for standing, top, bottom, defence, and submissions.

Smart Training

Drill with purpose, spar with different partners, and stay consistent.

Mindset

Stay calm, breathe well, and trust the work you have already put in.

Competition Preparation Guide

1. Set Clear Goals

Understand why you are competing. Maybe you want to test your skills, gain experience, or simply challenge yourself. A clear purpose helps you stay focused and motivated.

2. Develop a Game Plan

A game plan gives you direction before the match starts. Keep it simple and based on what you do well.

Starting Position: Decide if you want to look for a takedown or pull guard.

Top Positions: Know your go-to guard pass and your best controls.

Bottom Positions: Have reliable sweeps, recoveries, and escapes ready.

Submissions: Focus on a few high-percentage attacks you already understand well.

Defence: Be ready to protect yourself, stay composed, and work back to safety.

3. Train Smart

Consistency matters more than trying to do everything at once. Use training time with purpose.

Spar with Different Partners: This helps you adapt to different styles, sizes, and reactions.

Drill Repeatedly: Repetition builds confidence and sharper timing.

Conditioning: Add cardio, bodyweight work, and mobility so you can keep a strong pace.

4. Mental Preparation

Your mindset can make a big difference on competition day.

Relax and Breathe: Control your breathing to stay calm and avoid burning energy too early.

Visualization: Picture yourself following your plan, staying composed, and responding well under pressure.

Stay Positive: Trust your preparation and avoid feeding doubts before the match.

5. Understand the Rules

Learn the event rules and point system before the tournament. Know what is legal at your level and how points are scored.

Takedown or Throw: 2 points

Sweep: 2 points

Knee on Belly: 2 points

Passing the Guard: 3 points

Mount Position: 4 points

Rear Mount Position: 4 points

6. Gear Up

Make sure you have everything ready before the day arrives.

Gi: Check that your gi meets the tournament requirements and is clean and in good condition.

Belt: Wear the belt that matches your rank.

Mouthguard: Bring one if required or if you prefer the extra protection.

Extras: Pack water, snacks, a spare uniform if needed, and any personal items you rely on.

7. Warm Up Properly

A familiar warm-up helps your body feel ready and helps your mind settle.

Routine: Use movements you already know from training.

Focus: Stay present and pay attention to your breathing and movement quality.

8. Nutrition and Hydration

Good fuel supports good performance.

Hydrate: Drink enough water in the days leading up to the competition.

Eat Smart: Choose foods that give you energy without making you feel heavy on match day.

9. On Competition Day

Try to arrive early and keep things simple.

Arrival: Check the venue, find your mat area, and understand the event flow.

Warm Up Again: Do a light warm-up before you compete so your body is ready to move.

10. Reflect Afterward

Every match is useful experience, no matter the result.

Review Your Performance: Think about what worked well and what needs more attention in training.

Set New Goals: Use the experience to keep improving and preparing for the next challenge.
